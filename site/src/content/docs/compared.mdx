---
title: arcsec compared with ASTAP
description: How often arcsec and ASTAP's command-line solver solve, how accurately and how fast, on 635 test images with known answers - with a position hint and blind.
---

import { Aside } from '@astrojs/starlight/components';

arcsec and ASTAP's command-line solver, `astap_cli`, were run on the same 635 test
images, given the same position hint, field size and star databases, and scored the
same way. The images have known correct solutions, so every answer could be checked,
not just counted.

Measured on 12–13 October 2026: arcsec 0.7.0 against `astap_cli` CLI-2026.07.30, both
with the D80, G05 and W08 databases.

## At a glance

596 of the images can be solved; the other 39 are controls (below). A solve counts only
if all four corners of the image land within 5″ of the truth (or one pixel, on very
coarse images); a solution further out than that counts as a wrong answer.

ASTAP is shown twice: with its default settings, which is how N.I.N.A. and SharpCap run
it unless told otherwise, and with `-z 1`, which turns off its automatic downsampling of
large images. That setting is the one change that helped it most.

| | arcsec | ASTAP, defaults | ASTAP, `-z 1` |
|---|---|---|---|
| Solved correctly (of 596) | **589 (99 %)** | 303 (51 %) | 339 (57 %) |
| Wrong answers | 1 | 2 | 2 |
| Controls answered (of 35 that must fail) | 0 | 0 | 0 |

arcsec solved every image that ASTAP solved, at either setting.

## Accuracy

Measured on the images both solvers solved, median error in arcseconds:

| | arcsec | ASTAP |
|---|---|---|
| Centre, against ASTAP's defaults (303 images) | 0.25″ | 0.26″ |
| Corners, against ASTAP's defaults (303 images) | **0.46″** | 0.71″ |
| Centre, against ASTAP `-z 1` (339 images) | 0.25″ | 0.26″ |
| Corners, against ASTAP `-z 1` (339 images) | **0.42″** | 0.63″ |

The two agree at the centre. At the corners, where an error in scale or rotation shows
most, arcsec is closer.

## Speed

Wall-clock time per image, start to finish, one image at a time on a 24-core Linux PC:

| | arcsec | ASTAP, defaults | ASTAP, `-z 1` |
|---|---|---|---|
| Median, images both solve | 0.10–0.15 s | 0.21 s | 0.28 s |
| Median, images that did not solve | 0.32–0.36 s | 0.66 s | 1.03 s |
| All 635 images, total | 162–196 s | 700 s | 1285 s |

arcsec ran in both runs and its own times differed between them, so the ranges show
how much the machine varied. Within each run, arcsec took about half ASTAP's time on
the images both solved, and gave up on an unsolvable image in a third to half the time.

|---|---|---|---|---|

On the real telescope frames the two are level. The difference is in the other three
sets, which cover a wider range of field sizes and image quality than most people's
own frames.

Most of the 2.5°–6° fields are coarse: 28 of the 43 are TESS images at 20″ per pixel,

setting, with G05 and W08 installed.

## Blind solving

With no position to start from, arcsec uses its [blind index](../catalogues/#the-blind-index),
built from the star database you already have. ASTAP has no index: given `-r 180`, as
N.I.N.A. sends for a blind solve, it searches the sky field by field.

Both were given a position on the opposite side of the sky with `-r 180`, so neither
could use it, and the right field size. The sample was 40 of the images, five from each
field-size band, chosen by a fixed rule before the run. arcsec had the default index
from D80 (287 MB, for fields of 0.3° and up).

| | n | arcsec | ASTAP |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fields of 0.3° and up: solved | 30 | **28** | 9 (and 2 inexact) |
| … median time when solved | | **1.3 s** | 39 s |
| Fields under 0.3°: solved | 10 | 0 | 0 (and 1 wrong answer) |
| Wrong answers | 40 | 0 | 1 |

On the 9 fields both solved, the median time was 1.1 s for arcsec and 39 s for ASTAP.
ASTAP's one wrong answer put a 0.17° field on the wrong part of the sky.

**Fields under 0.3°** are arcsec's weak spot in blind solving. The default index stops
at 0.3°, so for smaller fields arcsec falls back to searching the sky field by field,
which can run for many minutes; ASTAP gives up on them in under a second. A deeper index
(`arcsec catalog index build --min-fov 0.15`, 698 MB from D80) reaches down to 0.15°:
with it arcsec solved 2 of the 5 sample fields between 0.15° and 0.3°, in under a second
each, and still none below 0.15°. With a position hint, arcsec solves all 119 fields
under 0.3° (ASTAP 39, or 67 at `-z 1`).

<Aside>
These blind runs solved four images at a time, with arcsec limited to 4 threads per
image so that the machine was not overloaded. arcsec's own default uses every core, so
its blind times here are on the slow side. Each attempt was stopped after 10 minutes.
</Aside>

## Other differences

- **Wrong focal length.** With `--fov-search`, arcsec still solves when the scale it is
  given is up to four times too large or too small, for example a forgotten reducer.
- **No scale at all.** A blind solve does not need the field size either: without one,
  arcsec tries pixel scales from 0.3″ to 60″.
- **Image formats.** arcsec reads XISF, ASDF, JPEG, PNG and TIFF as well as FITS.
- **Catalogues.** `arcsec catalog recommend` and `arcsec catalog install` pick and
  fetch the right star database for your field, and build the blind index from it.
- **Embedding.** arcsec is also a [C library](../c-library/), which is how a Siril
  build can call it directly.

What arcsec does not do: ASTAP is also an image viewer, stacker and photometry tool,
and arcsec replaces only its command-line solver. The two can share one set of star
databases.

## How this was measured

<Aside type="caution" title="Read these numbers with this in mind">
The test images and the scoring script were made by arcsec's authors, and the images
were chosen to find arcsec's weak spots; arcsec has been improved against them since,
and ASTAP has not. Most of them are survey images rather than frames from amateur
cameras. Read the results as where the two solvers differ, not as how often either will
solve your own frames.
</Aside>

- **The images.** 635 FITS images with known solutions: 42 telescope frames from Las
  Cumbres Observatory with their pipeline's solutions, 489 survey cutouts and frames,
  65 simulated camera problems, and 39 controls. The controls are 35 images that must
  not solve (noise, flat fields, fake star fields, shuffled fields, bright nebula cores,
  real fields with a hint 30°–60° away) and 4 given a deliberately wrong field size.
- **The same inputs for both.** Each solver was given the true centre as its position
  hint, the image height as `-fov`, and `-r 5`. ASTAP chose its own database by field
  size, as arcsec does.
- **Defaults otherwise.** Apart from `-z 1` in the second ASTAP run, neither solver
  was tuned. ASTAP might solve more with other settings for particular images, such as
  its star count (`-s`).
- **Scoring.** Each solution was checked at the centre and the four corners against
  the known answer.

The full method, every image and its source, and earlier results are in
[docs/test-images.md](https://github.com/cruzzil/arcsec/blob/main/docs/test-images.md)
in the repository. To repeat the comparison:

```sh
scripts/fetch-corpus.py
scripts/benchmark.py --corpus --auto-db --astap ~/astap_cli --jobs 1
```
